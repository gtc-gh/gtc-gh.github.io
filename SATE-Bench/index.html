Abstract

While Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) excel at zero-shot reasoning, their spatial capabilities are primarily evaluated through static, passive observation. In complex, human-centric environments, severe occlusions frequently obscure critical geometric evidence. Passive evaluations force models to hallucinate missing details rather than explicitly recognizing their own visual uncertainty. To bridge this gap, we introduce SATE-Bench, a novel interactive benchmark evaluating visual self-awareness and active exocentric view-exploration. Comprising 316 real-world scenes and 2,525 multi-turn QA pairs, SATE-Bench utilizes an Active/Passive diagnostic framework. In the Active setting, models start with a severely occluded view and must iteratively query adjacent perspectives to fully “sate” their need for definitive visual evidence, evaluated via a step-penalized reward metric to quantify exploration efficiency. Our comprehensive evaluation of state-of-the-art MLLMs reveals critical deficits: current models underestimate their spatial uncertainty, prematurely terminate exploration, and struggle significantly with fine-grained 3D geometric tasks. Furthermore, we explore eliciting visual self-awareness via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), demonstrating that while reinforcement learning successfully incentivizes active view-seeking behaviors, the continuous viewpoint-switching exposes bottlenecks in dynamic multi-view fusion. These findings indicate that scaling passive multi-view capabilities does not intrinsically yield active spatial reasoning, highlighting an urgent need for models that intelligently seek out necessary spatial views.

Benchmark

The SATE-Bench Benchmark

We source dense, synchronized 360° multi-view captures from ten human-centric datasets, sort the views clockwise around each scene, and annotate challenging multiple-choice questions. For the Active setting, annotators choose an initial view that genuinely requires exploration; for the Passive setting, they choose a subset of views that guarantees the question is answerable. Two independent reviewers cross-validate every item.

Two diagnostic settings

Active 1,615 questions
One occluded initial view. At each turn the model answers or moves to the adjacent left (X) or right (Y) view, keeping the full history. Scored by accuracy, steps, active rate and the step-penalized reward.
Passive 910 questions
A bag of annotated views that contains all the evidence. Isolates multi-view fusion from exploration.

Findings

Key Findings

69.2 → 56.8

Passive–Active gap

Gemini-3.5-flash fuses static views well (69.2% Passive accuracy) but drops to 56.8% when it has to gather the views itself.

2.5 vs. 4.7

Premature termination

Humans explore in 88.1% of the questions and observe 4.7 views on average; the most exploratory model observes only 2.5, and models report high confidence when they stop.

31.9%

Geometry breaks down

Entity queries are handled reasonably well, but fine-grained 3D tasks such as Human-Object Interaction collapse, even for the strongest model.

0.2% → 72.8%

RL elicits exploration

GRPO turns a model that almost never explores into one that explores most of the time, but stronger exploration rewards lower Active accuracy.

Training

Can MLLMs Learn to Explore?

We fine-tune Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct with Group Relative Policy Optimization on 19.3K single-view samples (a 1:1 mix of Active and Passive decisions) from M3GYM and Ego-Exo4D training scenes. The reward combines accuracy, format and an action term that encourages exploration.
